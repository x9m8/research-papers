# Number audit: Paper 12, "Paying for protein" (manuscript.md, version 1)

Auditor: independent number check (did not write the paper). Date: 28 October 2026.
Scope: every number in the abstract, social media summary, text, Tables 1–4, figure captions, Figures 1–3 and the graphical abstract. Checked against `results/*` and a full rerun from `data/raw`. Methods checked for errors that could change the conclusions.

## Summary verdict

**The numbers reproduce and are internally consistent. A few summary sentences overstate what the statistics show. No high-severity errors.**

- **Rerun:** I copied the package to `/home/claude/p12/audit_rerun` and ran it in the README order (01 → 02 → 03 → 04 → figures → graphical_abstract → tables). Every step exited 0. These outputs are byte-identical to the shipped files: `composition.json`, `prices.json`, `sample.json`, `twin_groups.csv`, `twin_groups_ratio.csv`, `whole_food_benchmarks.csv`, `tables.md`, `log_03.txt`, `log_04.txt` and `validation_sample.csv`. `breakeven_products.csv.gz` has the same decompressed md5. The rerun left the `## Tables` section of the manuscript unchanged.
- **Arithmetic:** I recomputed every figure in Tables 1–4 and every number in the text and abstract, including percentages, Wilson CI, BLS cost per 10 g protein, the median of the four benchmarks, every breakeven median and IQR (from protein per serving × US$/g), the twin ratios and the premium scenarios. All match the result files to the stated rounding.
- **Unit conversions:** These are correct. Dozen eggs = 12 × 50 g edible (SR Legacy large egg). Gallon of milk = 4 × 976 g (244 g per cup). Per lb = 453.59 g. Yogurt is 227 g per 8 oz. The BLS item titles in `ap.item` match the stated units, and the SR Legacy fdc_ids match the stated foods. The October 2025 value is missing in all 11 series, so n = 11 months is correct.
- **Breakeven formula:** It is correct: label protein (g) × benchmark US$/g. Twin premium: r = median protein per 100 g (protein-named) / median (other), and the breakeven premium is r − 1. Protein per dollar at premium p is r/(1 + p), which is correct. The Holm adjustment code is correct. Pooling in C1 uses weights equal to n exposed, and the comparator is non-E2 (also for the E1 sensitivity), as described.
- **Main issues:** The social media summary, the Conclusion and the graphical abstract say that above about 49 cents a serving *all four* whole foods supply protein more cheaply. That is not what was computed. US$0.49 is the median breakeven against the *median* of the four benchmarks. For a median product (15 g/serving) the chicken breakeven is US$0.61 and the whole-milk breakeven is US$0.51, so at 49–60 cents chicken and milk are *not* cheaper. The abstract's "premium below 66%" sentence applies to the median brand line only.

## Methods checks

1. **Product selection by name or brand (E2/E1).** The regex is applied to description + brand + sub-brand. I found very few name-level false positives: 1 "low protein" and 3 "no protein" among 6,011. Precision is supported by the validation sample. Recall against front-of-pack claims cannot be measured, and the manuscript says so. No change to conclusions.
2. **Within-category comparison (C1).** Implemented as described. One caveat the paper does not discuss: powders (per 100 g) and ready-to-drink products (per 100 ml) share categories, which inflates per-100 g contrasts. Restricting the pooled protein contrast to category × nutrient-basis strata gives 20.9 g instead of 22.9 g (point estimate, audit rerun). The per-100 kcal and per-10 g-protein outcomes do not depend on dilution. Conclusions unchanged. Optional: one sentence in the limitations.
3. **Same-brand-line comparison (C2/B3).** In 26 of 594 twin groups the protein-named and other products use a different nutrient basis (e.g. a powder vs an RTD). Excluding them, the median ratio is 1.67 (541 groups), 65.1% stay within the limit at a 31% premium and 56.4% at 50%. Essentially unchanged.
4. **Breakeven formula and premium scenarios:** correct (see the summary). The 31% and 50% figures are clearly framed as non-US scenarios.
5. **BLS unit conversions and protein per unit:** correct. Protein quality is not corrected, as acknowledged. Cooking losses are ignored, as acknowledged.
6. **Labelled servings:** protein per serving = protein/100 g × serving (g or ml), rounded to whole grams. Serving units other than g or ml are set to missing (5,914 of 6,011 E2 kept). The upper-bound framing is appropriate.
7. **Missing added sugars:** treated as missing, not zero. The post-2023 sensitivity analysis (≈61% coverage in both groups) gives −3.43 vs −3.61 g/100 kcal. I could not check whether some sources code a missing value as 0, because the extract does not distinguish them.
8. **Package weights:** parsing was spot-checked on 100 E2 records across 4 categories. It correctly takes the metric amount from "oz/g" strings. Most strings describe the unit sold. The per-package results are rightly secondary.
