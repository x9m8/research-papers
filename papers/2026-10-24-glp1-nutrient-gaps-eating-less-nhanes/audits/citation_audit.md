Manuscript audited: `/home/claude/p9/pkg/manuscript/manuscript.md` (not edited).
Audit date: 21 October 2026. Auditor: independent AI agent (Claude), working separately from the drafting session.

- NHANES documentation pages on wwwn.cdc.gov; the NCBI Bookshelf DRI tables (NBK545442); the National Academies "read online" text for IOM 2000 (NAP 9956) and NRC 1986 (NAP 618); DailyMed labels for Wegovy (setid ee06186f-…, revised 6/2026) and Zepbound (setid 487cd7e7-…, revised 8/2026); and the Healio article.

## 1. Summary of findings

| Severity | Issue |
|---|---|
| **Major** | **Novelty claim is wrong.** Fulgoni et al. (2024, *J Nutr* 154(9):2732–2742) already simulated proportional caloric reductions (20–50%) in NHANES 2015–2018 adults with overweight or obesity and estimated the share below the EAR or above the AI, with and without supplements. The sentence "I am not aware of a published simulation of this kind" (Intro, para 3) must be replaced with a statement that cites and differentiates this work. |
| Major | **Frankenfeld 2026 title is wrong** in the reference list. The actual title is "Analysis of NHANES: Nutrient, Food, and Beverage Intake …". The manuscript's expansion, "National Health and Nutrition *Survey*", also misnames NHANES. |
| Moderate | **The Wegovy/Zepbound eligibility statement (§2.1) has no citation, and the current labels do not state BMI cut-offs in the indication.** The BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a comorbidity, criteria appear only in the trial descriptions (Section 14). Add the label references and reword. |
| Moderate | **The Healio reference has no author, and the in-text citation does not match it.** The author is Michael Monostra. The finding was reported before the congress as Abstract 1823 ("to be presented", ECO, 12–15 May 2026). |
| Minor | van Can: the ~16% reduction was measured at an ad libitum **lunch**, and both 1.8 mg and 3.0 mg produced it. The Introduction implies a whole-day reduction. |
| Minor | Gabe: −39.2 is a difference in **percentage points of relative change from baseline** versus placebo, not "39% lower intake". |
| Minor | NHANES: the claims about telephone recalls and no 24-hour supplement data come from the DR1TOT_L documentation, not RXQ_RX_L. Only a landing page is cited, and there is no reference entry for the 2017–March 2020 cycle. |
| Minor | AIs for potassium, fibre, choline and vitamin K, and the protein "reference body weight" basis, have no citation. All are in NASEM (2019) Appendix J (Tables J-2 to J-4). The NCBI table page carries no visible "Table J-1" label. |
| Minor | Joint advisory: the 1.2 g/kg target is not tied to a weight basis. The advisory says the basis is "unclear" in obesity, so applying it to reference weight is the author's choice and should say so. |
| Minor | AI interpretation: IOM (2000) frames the AI rule on the **group mean** ("mean usual intake at or above this level implies a low prevalence"). The manuscript reports the share of individuals at or above the AI. The wording should say that. |
| Minor | Missing DOIs for the National Academies books (10.17226/618, /9956, /25353). |

All 10 journal DOIs in the reference list resolve in Crossref, and their metadata match: volume, issue, pages and author lists are correct. The one title error is Frankenfeld.

## 5. Novelty findings

### Searches run (21 Oct 2026)
- **Europe PMC REST**, which covers MEDLINE/PubMed, PMC and preprints (medRxiv, bioRxiv, Research Square, Preprints.org, Qeios and others). Title/abstract-restricted queries: GLP-1/semaglutide/tirzepatide/incretin/anti-obesity medication AND (a) NHANES or "What We Eat in America" (33 hits); (b) "national survey", "dietary survey", NDNS, "food consumption survey" or "nutrition survey" (12); (c) "Estimated Average Requirement", "cut-point", "usual intake" or "nutrient adequacy" (6); (d) simulat*, scenario or modelled AND nutrient* (16). There was also a preprint-only query (SRC:PPR) on GLP-1 terms AND nutrient or diet terms (54 hits). A further query on "simulated caloric/energy/calorie reduction", or prorating/proportional/caloric reduction AND nutrient adequacy/EAR, returned 4 hits. All titles were screened, and candidate abstracts were read.
- **Citation tracking:** all 7 papers citing Fulgoni (2024) in Europe PMC were screened.
- **Web search** (general and extended): "simulation GLP-1 appetite reduction NHANES dietary recalls nutrient inadequacy Estimated Average Requirement…" and "GLP-1 nutrient adequacy modeled reduced energy intake national survey…".
- Google Scholar and the medRxiv site search were not queried directly. Europe PMC indexes medRxiv, which partly covers this gap.

### Result
1. **Direct precedent (must cite):** Fulgoni, Agler, Ricciuto, DiFrancesco, Williams & Hertzler (2024), *J Nutr* 154(9):2732–2742, doi:10.1016/j.tjnut.2024.07.022. This study applied proportional caloric reductions of 20%, 30%, 40% and 50% by "prorating daily calorie intake such that usual intakes of 14 nutrients were reduced proportional to caloric reduction". It used NHANES 2015–2018 adults ≥19 y with overweight or obesity, estimated the share below the EAR or above the AI, and repeated the analysis with dietary supplements. Results: "after a simulated 30% caloric reduction, 25%-40% of the population had intakes below the EAR for protein, vitamin B-6, and zinc, and 75%-91% of the population had intakes below the EAR for vitamin A, calcium, and magnesium (vs. 4%-18% and 45%-56%, respectively, without caloric reduction)". The framing is intentional dietary restriction, not GLP-1 therapy, and the abstract does not mention GLP-1. It is the same method family. Its results run in the same direction and are of similar size to the manuscript's.
2. **No GLP-1-specific simulation was found.** No published paper or preprint was found that applies *GLP-1 trial-measured* intake reductions to NHANES or another national dietary survey. The closest GLP-1 items are:
   - observational: Frankenfeld 2026 (NHANES users with diabetes); Johnson 2025 (n = 69); Ponzo 2025; Korus 2026;
   - conceptual: Hungund 2026 Research Square preprint (doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-10929531/v1), whose abstract mentions no NHANES, simulation or EAR;
   - perspective: Mogna-Peláez & Guasch-Ferré 2026, *J Nutr*, doi:10.1016/j.tjnut.2026.101684, "Avoiding Malnutrition in the Era of GLP-1…";
   - energy-balance modelling: Hubert et al. 2026, *J Acad Nutr Diet*, doi:10.1016/j.jand.2026.156366, which uses the Hall model for a single phenotype with no nutrients.
3. **Useful cross-check for population size (optional):** Wong, Karthikeyan & Fan applied STEP 1 and SURMOUNT-1 eligibility to NHANES 2015–2018 and found "an estimated population size of 93.0 million" (semaglutide; doi:10.1007/s10557-023-07488-3) and "93.4 million" (tirzepatide; doi:10.1007/s10557-024-07583-z). The trial criteria differ (STEP 1 excluded diabetes), so these are not directly comparable with the manuscript's 115 million. Only the abstracts were read.

### Suggested replacement for Intro ¶3, sentence 3
> "Fulgoni et al. (2024) showed, using NHANES 2015–2018, that prorating usual intake in adults with overweight or obesity by 20%–50% sharply raises the share below the EAR. I am not aware of a study that applies the intake reductions measured in GLP-1 trials, to the population eligible for GLP-1 obesity treatment, in post-pandemic NHANES data."

The Discussion should also compare the results with Fulgoni's. Their 30% scenario put 25–40% below the EAR for protein, B6 and zinc, and 75–91% below it for vitamin A, calcium and magnesium. These figures sit between the manuscript's 24% and 35% scenarios. The comparison also shows what the manuscript adds: GLP-1-anchored reductions, the eligibility definition, protein per kg of reference weight, the count of nutrients per person, and the 2021–2023 cycle. Fulgoni's conclusion that supplements narrowed but did not close the gaps, for all nutrients except protein, matches §3.4.
