## 1. Regulation and test method

### 1.1 Commission Regulation (EU) No 301/2014
- **Citation:** Commission Regulation (EU) No 301/2014 of 25 March 2014 amending Annex XVII to Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (REACH) as regards chromium VI compounds. OJ L 90, 26.3.2014, p. 1 (start page from header; end page not shown in the text read).
- **URL read:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0301 (full text)
- **Key content:**
  - Entry 47 para 5: leather articles coming into contact with the skin "shall not be placed on the market where they contain chromium VI in concentrations equal to or greater than 3 mg/kg (0,0003 % by weight) of the total dry weight of the leather."
  - Para 6: same limit for articles containing leather parts that contact the skin, applied per leather part.
  - Para 7: paras 5 and 6 "shall not apply to the placing on the market of second-hand articles which were in end-use in the Union before 1 May 2015."
  - Art. 2: applies from 1 May 2015. Entry into force 20 days after publication.
  - Recital 6: "The determination limit of the EN ISO 17075 standard method is 3 mg/kg (0,0003 % by weight)."
  - Recital 7: the limit is "expected to be 80 % effective in reducing the occurrence of new chromium VI-related" allergic dermatitis cases.
  - Recitals 1, 3, 9, 11 give the procedural history: Denmark submitted on 19 Jan 2012; RAC opinion 28 Nov 2012; SEAC opinion 6 Mar 2013; opinions sent to Commission 8 Apr 2013.
  - Recital 13: a 12-month period was allowed for stakeholders to comply, including for stock in the supply chain.
- **Note on scope:** the wording is "placed on the market". The only explicit exemption is for second-hand articles in end-use before 1 May 2015. There is no general exemption for stock: recital 13 explains that the 12-month deferral exists to cover it.

### 1.2 EN ISO 17075 test method
- **EVS-EN ISO 17075-1:2017**, Leather. Chemical determination of chromium(VI) content in leather. Part 1: Colorimetric method. Valid from 02.03.2017. Standard history lists EN ISO 17075:2008, dated 07.02.2008 (ISO 17075:2007).
  - URL read: https://www.evs.ee/en/evs-en-iso-17075-1-2017 (catalogue page, scope only)
  - Scope: method "suitable to quantify the chromium(VI) content in leathers down to 3 mg/kg."
- **EVS-EN ISO 17075-2:2017**, Part 2: Chromatographic method. Valid from 02.03.2017.
  - URL read: https://www.evs.ee/en/evs-en-iso-17075-2-2017 (catalogue page, scope only)
  - Scope: also quantifies "down to 3 mg/kg". Where both methods are used on the same sample, "the results obtained with this document are considered as the reference". Part 2 avoids interference from extract colour. Interlaboratory trials found the two methods comparable (Annex D).
- **Paper on method performance:** Davis SJ, Wise WR, Recchia S, Spinazzè A, Masi M (2021). The Evaluation of the Detection of Cr(VI) in Leather. *Analytica* 3(1):1–13. doi:10.3390/analytica3010001.
  - URL read: https://doaj.org/article/085b584805e14085ba45cdd3b5acacb9 (abstract)
  - Key point: "BS EN ISO 17075 part 1 was shown to be incapable of reliably resolving to 3 mg kg−1 or below in leather." Part 2 performs better but still cannot reliably detect 1 mg/kg. The abstract notes proposed EU rules that would lower the limit to 1 mg/kg.
- **RAC on the LOQ:** RAC (2012, item 1.5) stated that results below 3 mg/kg "show large variations and have limited reliability", and that a lower quantification limit would be possible with technical progress.

### 1.3 Earlier national limit: Germany
- **Citation:** Achtzehnte Verordnung zur Änderung der Bedarfsgegenständeverordnung vom 3. August 2010. BGBl. I Nr. 42 vom 13.08.2010, S. 1138.
- **URL read:** https://umwelt-online.de/recht/lebensmt/lebensmb.ges/z10_1138.htm (demo excerpt, truncated)
- **Key content:**
  - Covers leather articles intended for non-temporary skin contact: clothing, watch straps, bags and backpacks, chair covers, pouches, leather toys.
  - The rule is framed as a prohibition on processes that leave Cr(VI) detectable by the official method (Anlage 10 Nr. 8, method B 82.02-11, 2008-10). The text read gives no numeric value.
  - Entered into force the day after promulgation.
- **Related:** the Annex XV report and RAC opinion both refer to "the existing German restriction" (Aug 2010). RAC subtracted the cases it would already avoid (1,440–3,960 per year).
- **Earlier German detection-limit statement:** BfR press release, 04.07.2007 (via chemie.de): "Gegenwärtig liegt die analytische Nachweisgrenze bei rund 3 mg je kg Leder."
- **Denmark:** no national leather Cr(VI) limit was found. The Annex XV text read does not mention one, and says most Member States "have not established national restrictions." Treat a Danish national limit as unconfirmed. The Danish cement chromate rule (1983, max 2 ppm water-soluble Cr) is documented in Thyssen et al. 2009 (item 2.1).

### 1.4 Annex XV restriction report (Denmark, 2012)
- **Citation:** Danish Environmental Protection Agency (Danish Competent Authority for REACH). Annex XV Restriction Report: Proposal for a Restriction. Chromium (VI) compounds [in leather articles]. Version 1.0, 19 January 2012. Published by ECHA.
- **URL read:** https://chesar.echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/477b4727-e5fc-75da-ccd9-b8bcc2d2b7dd (full text, about 100k of 103k characters read; page numbers inferred from the extract)
- **Key numbers:**
  - About 44,000 new chromium allergy cases per year in the EU, 45% attributed to leather (p. 7). The restriction would be about 80% effective, "meaning 13,000 less cases per year" (A.2.3, p. 8). Footnote 2 says the dossier submitter corrected this figure after submission.
  - Prevalence: 0.2–0.7% of the EU population, about 1–3 million people (p. 6).
  - Copenhagen patch-test data, 1985–2007 (n = 16,228): 3.6% (1985) → 1% (1995) → 3.3% (2007) (p. 5).
  - Market surveys (pp. 30–32):

    | Survey | Samples | Result |
    |---|---|---|
    | Germany 2008 (BVL) | 588 | 23% above 3 mg/kg |
    | Germany 2009 (BVL) | 504 | 163 (32%) above 3 mg/kg |
    | Germany 2000–2006 (Länder) | about 850 | Cr(VI) detected in more than half |
    | Denmark 2002 (Rydin) | 43 | 15 (35%) above 3 mg/kg |
    | Denmark 2008/2011 (Johansen et al.) | 18 shoe pairs tested | 8 above 3 mg/kg |

  - Chrome-tanned share (p. 13): "According to the BREF draft 80-95% of the world tanneries use chromium (III) salts". TEGEWA estimates 80–85% of leather worldwide is chrome-tanned.
  - Formation chemistry (pp. 18–19): oxidation of Cr(III) is favoured by alkaline pH and by free radicals from oxidising fatty acids. "The oxidation by air may be favoured by high pH during the neutralisation or dyeing processes, photo-ageing and thermal ageing." The choice of fatliquor was "crucial". After ageing and UV, Cr(VI) was higher in outer layers. In a shoe study, 11 of 54 shoes initially below threshold showed Cr(VI) after 24 h at 80 °C.
  - RAPEX: not mentioned in the portion read.

### 1.5 ECHA consultation note on the Danish proposal (2012)
- **URL read:** https://chesar.echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/f03030f4-eb9f-7f6b-5a99-6d0f7172e9d3 (full note)
- **Key content:**
  - "more than 30% of the tested articles of leather contained chromium VI in concentrations above 3 mg/kg" (DE and DK surveys).
  - 0.2–0.7% of the EU population allergic.
  - About 45% of new cases linked to leather.
- **Discrepancy:** this note was summarised as saying about 4,000 cases per year would be avoided. That conflicts with the Annex XV figure (13,000) and the RAC figure (below). Cite the RAC opinion or Annex XV, not this note, for case numbers.

### 1.6 RAC/SEAC opinion (2012–2013)
- **Citation:** ECHA Committee for Risk Assessment (RAC) and Committee for Socio-economic Analysis (SEAC). Compiled RAC opinion and draft SEAC opinion on an Annex XV dossier proposing restrictions on chromium (VI) compounds in leather articles. ECHA/RAC/RES-O-0000001412-86-09/F. 4 December 2012 (RAC adopted 28 Nov 2012).
- **URL read:** https://chesar.echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/e55bb713-4100-018b-ac13-ad1070658e75 (full text)
- **Key numbers:**
  - Patch-test prevalence in clinics: 2.3% (DK 2005) to 6.1% (DE 2007). Danish clinic average 2004–2010: 2.96%.
  - General population: RAC medium estimate 0.32%. EU sensitised population 0.84–2.31 million.
  - New cases: 20,000–55,000 per year EU-wide (median 37,500). Attributable to leather (45%): 9,000–24,750 per year.
  - Cases avoided: 7,200–19,800 per year. After subtracting the German restriction effect: 5,760–15,840 per year (mean 10,800).
  - Market data: "at least 30 % of the articles tested" in DK/DE surveys over 10 years exceeded 3 mg/kg. German survey means were 13.7 mg/kg (gloves) and 12.7 mg/kg (footwear).
  - The 80% effectiveness figure is expert judgement by the dossier submitter.
  - RAC assumed 30% of measured Cr(VI) migrates to skin.
  - Method: EN ISO 17075:2007. Below 3 mg/kg, results have "limited reliability".

## 2. Epidemiology of chromium allergy

### 2.1 Thyssen et al. 2009
- **Citation:** Thyssen JP, Jensen P, Carlsen BC, Engkilde K, Menné T, Johansen JD (2009). The prevalence of chromium allergy in Denmark is currently increasing as a result of leather exposure. *Br J Dermatol* 161(6):1288–1293. doi:10.1111/j.1365-2133.2009.09405.x
- **URL read:** Europe PMC REST core record (abstract), via Chrome: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1111/j.1365-2133.2009.09405.x%22&format=json&resultType=core. Also read: https://research.regionh.dk/en/publications/the-prevalence-of-chromium-allergy-in-denmark-is-currently-increa/
- **Key numbers:**
  - n = 16,228 patch-tested patients, 1985–2007.
  - Prevalence fell from 3.6% (1985) to 1% (1995) (P-trend < 0.001), then rose to 3.3% (2007) (P-trend < 0.001).
  - Relevant cement exposure fell from 12.7% (1989–94) to 3.0% (1995–2007).
  - Relevant leather exposure rose from 24.1% to 45.5% (P < 0.02).

### 2.2 Carøe et al. 2010
- **Citation:** Carøe C, Andersen KE, Thyssen JP, Mortz CG (2010). Fluctuations in the prevalence of chromate allergy in Denmark and exposure to chrome-tanned leather. *Contact Dermatitis* 63(6):340–346. doi:10.1111/j.1600-0536.2010.01798.x
- **URL read:** same Europe PMC REST call as 2.1 (abstract)
- **Key numbers:**
  - Odense data, 1992–2009, n = 8,483. 231 chromate-allergic patients.
  - "No significant changes in the prevalence or exposure sources" were found. There was a non-significant tendency to increase from 1997.
  - Leather shoes were the most frequent exposure source (24.4%). Foot dermatitis occurred in 40.3% and hand eczema in 60.6%.

### 2.3 Thyssen, Menné, Johansen 2014 (editorial)
- **Citation:** Thyssen JP, Menné T, Johansen JD (2014). Hexavalent chromium in leather is now regulated in European Union member states to limit chromium allergy and dermatitis. *Contact Dermatitis* 70(1):1–2. doi:10.1111/cod.12182
- **URL read:** same Europe PMC REST call (metadata only; editorial, no abstract)

### 2.4 Bregnbak et al. 2014 (pre-regulation baseline)
- **Citation:** Bregnbak D, Thyssen JP, Zachariae C, Johansen JD (2014). Characteristics of chromium-allergic dermatitis patients prior to regulatory intervention for chromium in leather: a questionnaire study. *Contact Dermatitis* 71(6):338–347. doi:10.1111/cod.12291
- **URL read:** https://research.regionh.dk/en/publications/characteristics-of-chromium-allergic-dermatitis-patients-prior-to/ (abstract)
- **Key numbers:**
  - 155 Cr-allergic patients compared with 621 controls.
  - 66% of Cr-allergic patients had a history of dermatitis caused by leather.
  - Cr-allergic patients had lower quality of life (p < 0.001) and more sick leave (p = 0.007).

### 2.5 Bregnbak et al. 2015 (review)
- **Citation:** Bregnbak D, Johansen JD, Jellesen MS, Zachariae C, Menné T, Thyssen JP (2015). Chromium allergy and dermatitis: prevalence and main findings. *Contact Dermatitis* 73(5):261–280. doi:10.1111/cod.12436
- **URL read:** https://research.regionh.dk/da/publications/chromium-allergy-and-dermatitis-prevalence-and-main-findings/ (abstract)
- **Key content:** a review of chemistry, exposure sources, regulation and epidemiology. It calls for continued surveillance of exposure sources and prevalence. The abstract gives no prevalence numbers.

### 2.6 Alinaghi et al. 2019 (trend through 2017)
- **Citation:** Alinaghi F, Zachariae C, Thyssen JP, Johansen JD (2019). Temporal changes in chromium allergy in Denmark between 2002 and 2017. *Contact Dermatitis* 80(3):156–161. doi:10.1111/cod.13181
- **URL read:** https://research.regionh.dk/da/publications/temporal-changes-in-chromium-allergy-in-denmark-between-2002-and-/ (abstract)
- **Key numbers:**
  - 13,379 adults, overall prevalence 2.2%.
  - The prevalence trend decreased (P-trend = 0.00002). The periods 2010–2013 and 2014–2017 were each significantly lower than 2002–2005.
  - Clinically relevant leather exposure rose from 42.3% (2002–2009) to 54.8% (2010–2017) (P = 0.04).
- **Caveat for citing:** the abstract calls the 2015 measure an "EU directive". It is a Regulation. The decline began before the 2015 application date.

### 2.7 Alinaghi (Seyed-Alinaghi) et al. 2021
- **Citation:** Alinaghi F, Thyssen JP, Zachariae C, Johansen JD (2021). No immediate effect of regulatory reduction of chromium in leather among adult patients with chromium allergy. *Contact Dermatitis* 85(5):514–522. doi:10.1111/cod.13925. PMID 34185877.
- **URL read:** https://research.regionh.dk/da/publications/no-immediate-effect-of-regulatory-reduction-of-chromium-in-leathe/ (abstract). Author name confirmed as "Alinaghi" via Crossref listing.
- **Key numbers:**
  - Questionnaire, Aug 2019: 172 Cr-allergic patients and 587 controls. Response rate 61.2% (759/1,241).
  - Leather-caused dermatitis in 71.0% (diagnosed 2003–2012) vs 66.2% (2013–2018), P = 0.5.
  - Foot dermatitis OR 3.82 (95% CI 2.07–7.08). Hand dermatitis OR 1.98 (1.13–3.49).
  - Conclusion: "No immediate sign of improvement" in the 5 years after adoption. The regulation "may have to be revised."

### 2.8 Larsen et al. 2026 (the key post-2015 trend study)
- **Citation:** Larsen CK, Jensen MB, Isufi D, Zachariae C, Johansen JD, Schwensen JFB (2026). Trends in Contact Allergy to Chromium Following the 2015 European Union Leather Regulation. *Contact Dermatitis* 94(1):67–72. doi:10.1111/cod.70045. Published online 3 Nov 2025; print Jan 2026.
- **URL read:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/cod.70045 (Crossref record including the abstract; Wiley page failed to load)
- **Key numbers:**
  - Gentofte Hospital, 2016–2024, n = 4,830 consecutive adults patch-tested with potassium dichromate 0.5% pet.
  - Prevalence 2.1%, "stable trends across test years".
  - No significant difference from earlier Gentofte data: 2006–2009 (2.3%, p = 0.65) and 2010–2013 (1.9%, p = 0.41).
  - Leather shoes were the most frequent exposure.
  - Conclusion: the 2015 regulation does not yet appear to have significantly reduced prevalence.

### 2.9 General-population prevalence statement
- Hedberg et al. 2015 (item 3.1) and Mathiason et al. 2015 (item 3.2): "Approximately 1–3% of the adult population in Europe" are allergic to Cr, citing Thyssen & Menné 2010 (*Chem Res Toxicol* 23:309–318).
- Hedberg 2020 (item 3.3) says "around 1% of the general population."
- These figures differ from the ECHA estimates (0.2–0.7% in Annex XV; 0.32% in RAC). Flag the discrepancy if citing.

## 3. Chemistry: Cr(VI) formation in chrome-tanned leather

### 3.1 Hedberg, Lidén, Odnevall Wallinder 2015 (Part I)
- **Citation:** Hedberg YS, Lidén C, Odnevall Wallinder I (2015). Chromium released from leather – I: exposure conditions that govern the release of chromium(III) and chromium(VI). *Contact Dermatitis* 72(4):206–215. doi:10.1111/cod.12329. PMCID PMC4415597.
- **URL read:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4415597/ (full text, via Chrome)
- **Key statements:**
  - "More than 90% of the leather produced worldwide (∼ 2 billion m2) is Cr-tanned" (refs: Thanikaivelan 2005; Beghetto 2013; Covington 1997).
  - "Between 7% and 50% of ∼ 9500 leather products tested and reported since the year 2000" contained Cr(VI) above 3 mg/kg.
  - Heating, often above 80 °C, during manufacture or shoe making can increase Cr(VI) in the presence of certain fatty acids and oxygen. UV under dry conditions acts similarly. Shoe making with dry heat and alkaline glues can trigger Cr(VI).
  - Relative humidity above 35% and higher water content reduce Cr(VI).
  - Cr(III) release far exceeds Cr(VI): above 3,000 mg/kg after 168 h in artificial sweat for glove leather.
  - Several ISO 17075 parameters (grinding, temperature, duration) influence the result.

### 3.2 Mathiason, Lidén, Hedberg 2015 (Part II)
- **Citation:** Mathiason F, Lidén C, Hedberg YS (2015). Chromium released from leather – II: the importance of environmental parameters. *Contact Dermatitis* 72(5):275–285. doi:10.1111/cod.12334. PMCID PMC4964937. PMID 25630767.
- **URL read:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4964937/ (full text, via Chrome)
- **Key statements:**
  - "Relative humidity (water content in leather) during storage prior to Cr extraction was the single most important parameter." Cr(VI) varied "from non‐detectable to levels significantly exceeding the restriction limit" depending on RH.
  - Cr(VI) was undetectable at RH above 35% (at 70 °C pre-storage). For the leather tested, only 50% and 80% RH gave results below 3 mg/kg.
  - Contact with alkaline solution (pH 12) and UV during storage increased Cr(VI).
  - "The amount of Cr(VI) in leather is not an intrinsic property". The same leather "can give a negative test result, a positive test result, or a very positive test result."
  - A sample "will test positive in a dry and cold country" more readily than in a humid one. The authors recommend conditioning at about 20% RH for at least 24 h before testing.
  - Cr(III) MET10% is 0.18 µg/cm² vs Cr(VI) 0.03 µg/cm² (Hansen et al. 2003).
- **Relevance:** directly supports the argument that notification counts depend on test conditions and climate, not only on product quality.

### 3.3 Hedberg 2020 (review)
- **Citation:** Hedberg YS (2020). Chromium and leather: a review on the chemistry of relevance for allergic contact dermatitis to chromium. *Collagen and Leather* 2(1) [article number not shown]. doi:10.1186/s42825-020-00027-y.
- **URL read:** https://journal.hep.com.cn/cal/EN/10.1186/s42825-020-00027-y (abstract). The Springer page and the PDF failed to load.
- **Key statements:**
  - Cr(VI) forms on some antioxidant-free leathers under dry conditions (below 35% RH), influenced by tanning, UV, alkaline contact and leather age.
  - Cr(III) is the dominant species released.
  - A low initial ISO 17075 result (below 3 mg/kg) "does not guarantee" low release over the service life.
  - Labels and certificates are often insufficient for allergic people.

### 3.5 Share of chrome-tanned leather: citable sources
- **BREF 2013 (primary EU source).** Black M, Canova M, Rydin S, Scalet BM, Roudier S, Delgado Sancho L (2013). *Best Available Techniques (BAT) Reference Document for the Tanning of Hides and Skins*. EUR 26130 EN. Publications Office of the EU. ISBN 978-92-79-32947-0. doi:10.2788/13548.
  - URLs read: https://aida.ineris.fr/sites/aida/files/documents-bref/TAN_BREF_022013.pdf (full text, truncated after s. 2.3.3) and https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC83005 (metadata).
  - p. 10: "Of all world tanneries, 80 – 90 % use chromium(III) salts in their tanning processes." This is a share of tanneries, not of leather output.

  - Hedberg et al. 2015 (3.1): ">90% of the leather produced worldwide".
- **Recommendation:** cite the BREF as "80–90% of world tanneries", and note the range of other estimates.

## 4. Prior analyses of RAPEX / Safety Gate data

### 4.1 Pigłowski 2023
- **Citation:** Pigłowski M (2023). Notifications in European Rapid Alert system for dangerous products (RAPEX). In: *Safety and Reliability of Systems and Processes, Summer Safety and Reliability Seminar 2023*, pp. 187–198. Gdynia Maritime University. doi:10.26408/srsp-2023-14.
- **URL read:** https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-2cdfba2b-b2e7-4594-8ccf-d1abea470f02/c/piglowski_notifications_2023.pdf (full text)
- **Key numbers:**
  - 2005–2021: 25,659 notifications analysed (85% of all; multi-risk notifications excluded).
  - Chemical risk accounted for 26.1%, second after injuries (26.7%).
  - Notifying countries: DE 15.4%, ES 8.6%, HU 8.1%, FR 6.7%, UK 6.1%, BG 5.6%.
  - Origin: China 53.8%, unknown 8.4%.
  - Cr(VI) and leather are not separately analysed.

### 4.2 Pigłowski 2018
- **Citation:** Pigłowski M (2018). Notifications of dangerous products from European Union countries in the RAPEX as an e-service. *European Journal of Service Management* 26(2):175–183. doi:10.18276/ejsm.2018.26-22.
- **URL read:** https://wnus.usz.edu.pl/ejsm/de/issue/1158/article/18303/ (abstract)
- **Key numbers:** 2005–2016, China accounted for 57% of notifications and EU origins for 18%. No chromium content.

### 4.3 Kawakami, Isama, Ikarashi 2013 (closest methodological analogue)
- **Citation:** Kawakami T, Isama K, Ikarashi Y (2013). [Characterization of cases contravening of regulations regarding primary aromatic amines originating from azo dyes in commercial textile products and leather products in European Union]. *Kokuritsu Iyakuhin Shokuhin Eisei Kenkyusho Hokoku* (131):66–74. PMID 24340672. In Japanese, with English abstract.

### 4.4 Michalek et al. 2019

### 4.6 Vincze, Al Dahouk, Dieckmann 2019

- **URL read:** listed in Europe PMC search results (title and metadata only; abstract not read)

### 4.7 RIVM 2013 (critique of RAPEX as a data source)
- **Citation:** Wijnhoven SWP, Janssen PJCM, Schuur AG (2013). *Definition of serious risk within RAPEX notifications*. RIVM Letter Report 090013001/2013. Bilthoven: RIVM.
- **URL read:** https://www.rivm.nl/bibliotheek/rapporten/090013001.pdf (full text)
- **Key content:**
  - In 2011, chemical risks were 19% of EU notifications (347/1,803) but 62.5% of Dutch notifications (25/40).
  - Chromium VI is "frequently notified in leather products such as gloves and jackets."
  - Member States interpret "serious risk" for chemicals differently. Exceeding a limit is not proof of risk, because limits are often set for risk management reasons.
  - "Some measures taken against dangerous products do not result in notifications."
  - Participation depends on national surveillance organisation, country size and market structure. The Dutch authority (NVWA) notifies at a two-fold limit exceedance.
- **Why it matters:** the best source found for enforcement and reporting bias between Member States.

## 6. EU description of Safety Gate data

### 6.1 Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2019/417 (RAPEX guidelines)
- **Citation:** Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2019/417 of 8 November 2018 laying down guidelines for the management of the European Union Rapid Information System 'RAPEX' established under Article 12 of Directive 2001/95/EC... OJ L 73, 15.3.2019, p. 121. No longer in force from 26.3.2025 (EUR-Lex metadata).
- **URLs read:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019D0417 (first 100k of 144k characters) and https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019D0417 (metadata)
- **Notification criteria:**
  - Article 12 notifications require that the product is subject to measures that "prevent, restrict or impose specific conditions" on marketing or use, plus serious risk and possible cross-border effect.
  - Article 11 covers products posing "a less than serious risk".
- **Chemicals (s. 3.4.3.3):** "The risk level of a product may be considered to be serious if it contains a chemical substance either banned" or "in a concentration above the limit established by European legislation". In such cases "a notification may be submitted without a detailed risk assessment."
- **Relevance:** Cr(VI) leather alerts are essentially limit-exceedance notifications.

### 6.2 BAuA (German national contact point)
- **URL read:** https://www.baua.de/EN/Topics/Monitoring-evaluation/Market-surveillance-and-product-safety/Market-surveillance/Safety-Gate
- **Key content:**
  - Safety Gate is "the European Union reporting system for dangerous consumer goods, excluding food, medications and medical devices."
  - The GPSR, Regulation (EU) 2023/988, replaces Directive 2001/95/EC from 13 Dec 2024.
  - Notifications cover authority and voluntary measures (withdrawal, recall).
  - The risk method rates severity and probability on four levels: serious, high, medium, low.
  - The page itself contains a typo in the decision number. Use 2019/417 (6.1).
