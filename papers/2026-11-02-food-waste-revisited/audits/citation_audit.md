# Citation audit: Paper 19, "US food waste revisited: a reproduction with public data"

Auditor: independent citation audit (Claude, claude-opus-5-5), 29 October 2026. I did not write the manuscript and did not edit it.
Files audited: `pkg/manuscript/manuscript.md`, `pkg/README.md`, `lit/brief.md`, plus the raw input files in `pkg/data/raw/` where a claim depended on them.

How sources were checked: Crossref REST API (`api.crossref.org/works/{DOI}`) for every DOI; the original article read in full on the PLOS ONE page and in the Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC2775916), with equations and inline formulas viewed as images on the PMC page (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2775916/) and Table 1 read from the XML table; abstracts through Europe PMC REST or Crossref; data pages fetched directly. All checks were made on 29 October 2026. My Chrome tab was closed at the end. I did not touch other tabs.

## 1. Summary

- **References: 18 of 18 exist** and the DOIs all resolve to the stated works, with matching authors, year, volume, issue and pages. There is one metadata fix: Jackson et al. (2002) appeared in *International Journal of Obesity and Related Metabolic Disorders*, the journal's name in 2002. It is the name the original article uses and the one APA 7 calls for. Crossref lists the current name. Smaller APA points are listed in §4.
- **In-text citations and reference list match one to one.** Every reference is cited and every citation has a reference. The only issues are style: APA 7 wants "Hall et al. (2009)" at first narrative citation, not "Hall, Guo, Dore and Chow (2009)", and the first parenthetical USDA ERS citation is missing its bracketed abbreviation.
- **Quotations from the original are verbatim.** I checked all six against the PLOS full text: the abstract sentence, "progressively increased from about 30%…", "within 5% of our calculation…", "push effect", "unless the uncertainties … at later dates" and the "∼10 kg" / "∼220" / "<10" / "∼9.5×10⁻⁴" statements. Numerical attributions are also correct: 9,100 kcal/kg and 22 kcal/kg/d (equation image), γF = 3.2 in the text against 3.6 ± 2 in Table 1, C = 10.4 kg, F0 = 19.1 kg and pW = 0.525 (inline formula), α = 0.54, I0 = 2,100 kcal/d from IOM sedentary, 10⁵ Monte Carlo runs, 1.9 kcal/g, 70% freshwater, 3 kcal fossil per kcal food.
- **Five statements about the original need correction or softening, mostly for fairness:**
  1. **Licence is wrong.** The manuscript (§Research transparency: "the original article (CC BY)") and the README/digitized-data header ("CC BY 4.0") call the article CC BY. It is released under the **Creative Commons Public Domain declaration (CC0 1.0)** (the license element in the PMC XML; the PLOS page says the same).
  2. **"Related work by some of the same authors … (Swinburn et al., 2009)".** Swinburn, Sacks and Ravussin share no author with Hall, Guo, Dore and Chow. Only Hall et al. (2011) shares authors (Hall, Chow). Swinburn's abstract also concerns "weight gain in the US population", meaning children and adults, not only adults.
  3. **"the US Department of Agriculture's own estimate" (§1).** In the original, the "USDA estimate of food waste" is a quantity Hall et al. constructed: FAO supply minus USDA loss-adjusted availability. §3.1 of the manuscript describes it correctly. §1 should match.
  4. **The reasons given for the comparisons that do not reproduce are asserted, not shown.** §3.1 says the USDA and EPA comparisons fail "because the comparison data have been revised", and §4.1 says "because both series have been revised". The audit did not compare the 2009 vintages, and the manuscript does not either. In addition, the EPA table has **no 2003 value** (it gives 2000 and 2005), so the 99 kg figure is interpolated. The original's ~30% also used a different source (EPA 2007 *Facts and Figures*, Table 9). This should be worded as "possibly because…", or the vintages should be compared.
  5. **"The supply plotted in the original Figure 1B … suggests a revision of the FAO data since 2009."** The original contradicts itself on this series. The Results text calls the open circles FAO supply. The Figure 1 caption calls them "Per capita U.S. food availability unadjusted (○) … according to the USDA". The inference therefore needs a caveat.
- **Two lesser fairness points:** §4.3 says the original "treated [the FAO supply] as a fixed reference". In fact the original discusses FAO uncertainty explicitly and rests its claim on the trend. §4.1 says the original's Monte Carlo intervals "already implied" the 32–44% range. That was not checked, and the original's intervals cover parameter uncertainty only.
- **Data-source claims are accurate.** These cover USDA ERS loss-adjusted availability (calories to 2010 only; file "Data last updated June 1, 2019"), EPA food data (1960–2019; 2018 methodology change), FAOSTAT FBSH and FBS (dataset names and DateUpdate values confirmed), NHANES (cycles through Aug 2021–Aug 2023), NCHS AD 347 Table 7, MMWR 53(4) and EIA MTTUPUS2. One count needs fixing: "NHANES has added ten more survey cycles" (see claims table, row C24).
- **Novelty: "I found no published reproduction" holds** on every search I could run. Searches covered OpenAlex: all 747 citing works, screened by title for replication terms plus energy-gap and US-waste terms. They also covered the FORRT FLoRA Replication Atlas (no record for the DOI), all I4R discussion papers 1–321 by title, PLOS reader comments and web searches. ReplicationWiki was unreachable (robots block and a site error page), so it is unverified. Later energy-gap or surplus estimates that include the US exist (Hiç et al., 2016; van den Bos Verma et al., 2020). They use requirement-based gaps, not the Hall body-weight model, and the manuscript already cites both. Suggested wording is in §5.

## 5. Novelty assessment

Question: does any published reproduction, replication, re-analysis or update of Hall et al. (2009) exist, and are there later US food-waste estimates by the energy-balance method?

Searches (29 Oct 2026):
1. **OpenAlex citing works** (`cites:W2037063633`, 747 records, all retrieved). The title/abstract search filter returned **0** for each of "replicat", "reproduc", "revisit", "reanaly" and "re-estimat". Title screens for comment/reply/critique/letter/overestimat/reassess/replic/reproduc found no critique of Hall 2009. The hits were the AJCN letter exchange on Swinburn 2009 (Hall & Chow, 2010, "Estimating the quantitative relation between food energy intake and changes in body weight", *AJCN* 91(3), 816; reply by Swinburn, Sacks & Ravussin, 817) and unrelated items. A screen of 56 titles on waste/loss/surplus plus US/energy/calorie terms, and of 37 on wastage/food supply/surplus, found US supply-chain loss studies (e.g., Buzby & Hyman 2012; Buzby et al. 2014 ERS; Dou et al. 2016, *Global Food Security* 8, 19–26, which is mass-based) and energy-imbalance-gap studies for the US, Japan, New Zealand and Belgium (intake gaps, not waste). **None reproduces or updates the US energy-balance waste calculation.**
2. **FORRT FLoRA Replication Atlas**, DOI page: "No replication data available" for 10.1371/journal.pone.0007940.
3. **Institute for Replication** discussion papers 1–321 (RePEc i4rdps, both listing pages) screened by title: nothing on food waste, Hall et al., or US food supply. The only nearby items are a replication of Macchi (2023) on obesity and a comment on a food-insecurity paper. Both are unrelated.
4. **ReplicationWiki**: unreachable (WebFetch blocked by robots; Chrome showed an error page). **Unverified.**
5. **Web searches** (extended) for the title plus replication/reanalysis/update/revisited, and for "Hall et al. (2009)" plus critique/overestimate/energy gap: only the original, press coverage, ReFED/NRDC supply-chain estimates and global energy-gap papers.
6. **PLOS reader comments**: two threads (a 2012 media-coverage thread; one reader comment of 28 Nov 2009 whose text I did not open). Not a reproduction.

Later estimates by a related method: Hiç et al. (2016) compute country-level food surplus (availability minus requirement from demography, body weight and activity), 1961–2010, which includes the US. van den Bos Verma et al. (2020) estimate consumer waste globally with an energy-gap approach. Both are requirement-based (steady-state) rather than Hall's dynamic body-weight model. The manuscript cites both, and its steady-state robustness variant is close in spirit to them. Neither is a reproduction of Hall et al.

**Verdict:** "I found no published reproduction" **holds**, with two caveats. ReplicationWiki could not be checked, and the OpenAlex screen used titles and abstracts, not full texts. A paper that re-runs the calculation inside its body text could therefore have been missed. Suggested wording: "I found no published reproduction or update of the US calculation (searches of OpenAlex citing works, the FLoRA Replication Atlas, Institute for Replication discussion papers and the web, 28–29 October 2026); related requirement-based energy-gap estimates exist for the world and for many countries, including the US (Hiç et al., 2016; van den Bos Verma et al., 2020)."
