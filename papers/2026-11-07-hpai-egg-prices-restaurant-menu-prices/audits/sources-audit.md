# Sources and citations audit — paper 23 ("Bird flu, egg prices and restaurant menu prices")

Independent verification via Crossref, FRED, and the live APHIS site (WebFetch/WebSearch; shell-level access to these hosts is blocked by the agent proxy, consistent with SOURCES.md).

## CONFIRMED issues

1. **APHIS data licensing claim is wrong/incomplete.** SOURCES.md and the manuscript treat APHIS dashboard data simply as "US government data" with an unread disclaimer. I read the disclaimer (aphis.usda.gov/map-data-disclaimer): it states the data are collected "for restricted government purposes only" and are "the sole property of MRPBS, APHIS or AMS," to be shared only with those who need them for their intended government purpose, subject to the Trade Secrets Act, Privacy Act, FOIA, and Food Security Act/2008 Farm Bill confidentiality provisions. This is materially different from an open public-domain statement and does not obviously authorize redistribution of extracts in a public reproducibility package. This should be flagged to the author/coordinator before the package is posted, as SOURCES.md itself suggests should happen ("the coordinator should confirm before posting") — but the manuscript's text treats this as settled ("US government data") without noting the restrictive language.

2. **AI-use disclosure names a model inconsistent with this environment.** The manuscript (§2.5, Acknowledgements) says analysis was done "with the assistance of Claude (Anthropic; model claude-sonnet-5-5)." This session's own model ID is "claude-sonnet-5" (per system environment), not "claude-sonnet-5-5" — the stated model name appears to be a fabricated/incorrect variant. Minor but should be corrected to the actual model identifier used.

## Checked and fine

3. **All 8 references verified real via Crossref**, with correct titles, authors, journals, and years: Chen, Pendell & Chang (2026, *Prev. Vet. Med.*, confirmed via Crossref bibliographic search after the direct DOI lookup intermittently failed — title/authors/journal/year all match exactly); Jordà (2005); Leibtag (2009); Mitchell, Thompson & Malone (2024); Montiel Olea & Plagborg-Møller (2021); Mutlu Çamoğlu, Serra & Gil (2014/2015 — Crossref's print-issue date is 2015-03, but "issued"/online date is 2014-12-15, so citing 2014 is a defensible, common convention, not an error); Newey & West (1987); Zamani, Bittmann & Ortega (2024).

4. **Newey–West page range: 703–708 is CORRECT, not an error.** The Econometric Society's own publications page (econometricsociety.org) lists the article at pages 703–708, Econometrica 55(3), May 1987, matching the manuscript's citation exactly. Crossref's omission of an end page is a Crossref metadata gap, not a citation error — no correction needed.

5. **No overclaiming found in title-only citations.** The manuscript explicitly and repeatedly hedges that it has read only titles/bibliographic records for Mitchell, Zamani, Chen, and Mutlu Çamoğlu, and every substantive claim attributed to them ("lags between flock losses and prices" for Mitchell; price-transmission/retail-premium framing for the others) is a safe paraphrase of the title itself. No instance asserts a specific finding, method, or magnitude beyond what the title supports.

6. **Novelty claim is reasonably supported.** Web search found no academic literature on egg-price-to-restaurant-menu-price CPI pass-through specifically. The closest related academic paper found (Thompson, "McImpact: Welfare Impacts of All-Day Breakfast after HPAI Outbreak," *American Journal of Management* 17(3), 2017) studies a demand-side welfare model around McDonald's all-day breakfast, not menu-price pass-through — it does not undercut the novelty claim. Other search results were news coverage of restaurant surcharges (anecdotal, not academic). The hedged phrasing ("I am not aware of... That statement rests on a limited literature search") is appropriate.

7. **APHIS dashboard and figures are plausible.** The live APHIS page confirms, verbatim, "more than 378.5 million egg-laying chickens in the United States" and the February 8, 2022 first-commercial-detection date used as the dashboard's start. A CRS report (everycrsreport.com, April 2025) independently states ~168.6 million total birds affected (all poultry types) by April 2025, with table-egg layers ~75% of poultry losses (~126.5M implied layers by April 2025) — consistent in scale and trajectory with the manuscript's running total of 152.4 million table-egg layers through September 2026 after 17 more months of accumulation (Table 2's annual breakdown sums consistently to that total). No evidence the 2026 end-date or scale is inconsistent with independently reported figures.

8. **BLS series IDs verified where checkable.** FRED titles confirm CUUR0000SEFV = "CPI-U: Food Away from Home, U.S. City Average"; APU0000708111 = "Average Price: Eggs, Grade A, Large (Cost per Dozen), U.S. City Average"; CUUR0000SAF11 = "CPI-U: Food at Home, U.S. City Average"; CES7072200008 matches the "Average Hourly Earnings of Production Employees, Food Services and Drinking Places" family (confirmed via state-level analogues; national series not independently pulled). CUUR0000SEFH, CUUR0000SA0, and CUURS12ASA0 were not independently confirmed via a title lookup but follow standard, well-documented BLS CPI item-code conventions (SEFH=eggs, SA0=all items, S12A=NY-Newark-Jersey City area code) consistent with the stated series.

9. **BLS licensing claim checks out.** BLS data are standard public-domain US federal statistical products; this is uncontroversial and not disputed by any source found.

10. **Methodology/AI disclosure otherwise consistent.** Software versions, retrieval dates (2–3 Nov 2026, matching "today" in this environment), and the description of Claude's role (data retrieval, code, reference checking, drafting, author review) are internally consistent and not contradicted by anything checked; only the specific model-name string (#2 above) is off.
